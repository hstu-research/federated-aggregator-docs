# Synthetic FedProx Thesis Executive Brief

**Status:** One-page, thesis-facing summary for supervisor or examiner review. This brief reports a bounded source-only governance contribution. It is not an empirical study, clinical evaluation, privacy assessment, deployment record, or operational readiness decision.

## Executive summary

This thesis addresses a governance problem in synthetic FedProx research: a proposed federated-learning protocol can be described as though it were an executed empirical result unless intent, implementation, and evidence are kept distinct. The contribution is a documentation-first, source-only boundary that represents proposed study intent as a frozen non-executing declaration; terminally closes unsafe, broadened, mutable, unknown, or replayed input; and retains empirical readiness as unassigned and ineligible.

The work establishes **local contract behavior**, not a model result. The completed artifacts include source-only validation and one-use consumption, in-memory terminal closure and restart suppression, a disabled application fake, an evidence matrix, thesis methodology and results drafts, a traceability appendix, a governance handoff brief, a submission-readiness audit, a manuscript assembly map, and a defense brief. Together they make the thesis contribution explicit and auditable without inferring results that do not exist.

> **Core message:** The thesis demonstrates how to distinguish proposed synthetic FedProx study intent from executed empirical evidence. It does not demonstrate FedProx performance, privacy in operation, clinical utility, hospital integration, deployment readiness, or runtime behavior.

## Research question

Can a descriptor-only synthetic FedProx study-intent declaration distinguish a proposed breast-cancer/FedProx protocol from an executed empirical result before any trainer, dataset, model, metric, update, submission, or aggregation action occurs?

## What is explicitly absent

No research dataset, patient record, medical image, model artifact, training run, inference, metric calculation, update, submission, aggregation, external adapter, remote workflow query, target action, hospital-system interaction, clinical evaluation, privacy outcome, deployment, or runtime proof entered this branch. Consequently, the thesis reports **no accuracy, loss, F1, AUC, convergence, communication, privacy, clinical, operational, or deployment result**.

## Recommended thesis interpretation

The appropriate thesis claim is that the work contributes an accountable, source-only governance boundary. It should be presented as a pre-empirical method for preventing category error—not as evidence that a federated model was built, tested, or clinically evaluated. Published FedProx, healthcare federated-learning, and governance literature should be used for context only; their results do not transfer to this project.

## Independent next decisions

Any empirical extension must be a separate, independently governed activity. Before it can be considered, the responsible parties must establish bounded research authority; applicable ethics, legal, consent, and data-stewardship decisions; a prespecified protocol and analysis plan; reproducibility, safety, and reporting controls; and a focused concrete-adapter review. Those decisions are **unassigned** in the present record.
