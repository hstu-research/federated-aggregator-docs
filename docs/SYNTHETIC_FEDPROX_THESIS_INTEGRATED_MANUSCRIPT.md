# Distinguishing Synthetic FedProx Study Intent from Empirical Results

## A Source-Only Governance Boundary for Breast-Cancer Research

**Manuscript status:** Integrated thesis manuscript for bounded source-only evidence. This manuscript is suitable for editorial and supervisory review of the documented governance contribution. It is not an empirical research report, clinical study, privacy assessment, deployment record, or submission decision.

## Abstract

Federated learning research can be misrepresented when a proposed protocol is described as though it were an executed experiment. This thesis examines a narrow research-governance question for a synthetic FedProx breast-cancer research branch: whether a descriptor-only study-intent declaration can distinguish a proposed protocol from an empirical result before any trainer, dataset, model, metric, update, submission, or aggregation action is available. The work adopts a documentation-first, source-only method. It defines frozen scalar declarations for a proposed non-executing posture, validates exact allowed state classes, terminally closes malformed, broadened, mutable, unknown, and replayed input, and keeps external workflow observation default-ineligible and unattempted. Deterministic in-memory fixtures and local source-quality checks provide bounded evidence about those controls.

The contribution is a traceable separation between **study intent** and **study execution**. The resulting artifacts provide a thesis-safe methodology, evidence matrix, limitations statement, traceability appendix, governance handoff, and claim audit. They prevent source-only contract behavior from being presented as a machine-learning, privacy, clinical, operational, deployment, or runtime finding. No research dataset, patient record, medical image, model artifact, training run, inference, metric calculation, update, submission, aggregation, remote workflow observation, hospital integration, or clinical evaluation was performed in this branch. Accordingly, this thesis reports no predictive performance, optimization behavior, or clinical utility. It instead provides an accountable governance foundation that a separately authorized empirical FedProx study would need to extend with a lawful data boundary, prespecified protocol, reproducible execution, and defined evaluation criteria. **Keywords:** Federated learning; FedProx; synthetic study intent; research governance; evidence boundaries; breast-cancer research; source-only validation; reproducibility.

# Chapter 1. Introduction

## 1.1 Background and problem statement

Federated learning is commonly discussed as a way to coordinate optimization across decentralized participants while retaining data locally. FedProx is an established optimization formulation proposed for heterogeneous federated settings; its published role is algorithmic context for the present thesis, not a result reproduced here. [1] Healthcare federated-learning literature also distinguishes technical prototypes from real-world translation, while governance scholarship emphasizes formal roles, controls, and accountable processes. [2] [3]

In such a setting, terminology matters. A project may contain a proposed algorithm, a code-level declaration, and preliminary documentation long before it has a lawful data boundary, a model, a training procedure, an evaluation plan, or a clinical setting. If these distinct states are not made explicit, a proposed protocol can be described as an executed result. This thesis addresses that category error at an earlier point than model development: the point at which study intent is recorded and constrained.

The resulting problem is therefore not whether FedProx achieves a particular performance level in breast-cancer classification. That question requires a separate empirical study. The present problem is whether a descriptor-only declaration can retain a proposed, non-executing posture in a way that is auditable and resistant to broadening, mutation, or replay before execution-shaped actions become available.

## 1.2 Research question and objectives

The thesis asks:

> **Can a descriptor-only synthetic FedProx study-intent declaration distinguish a proposed breast-cancer/FedProx protocol from an executed empirical result before any trainer, data, model, metric, update, or aggregation action occurs?**

| Objective | Operational meaning | Boundary retained |
|---|---|---|
| Represent study intent | Record a frozen declaration of a proposed, non-executing study posture. | No protocol execution, data access, or model use. |
| Prevent category error | Close malformed, broadened, mutable, unknown, or replayed declarations. | Closure is local contract behavior, not a scientific finding. |
| Preserve non-execution | Retain the absence of trainer, data/model, metric, update, submission, aggregation, and runtime actions. | Absence is not a privacy or clinical proof. |
| Separate external observation | Keep remote observation unattempted and default-ineligible. | No remote workflow was queried, classified, or changed. |

## 1.3 Scope and contribution

The thesis contributes a bounded **source-only governance boundary**. It does not propose a new federated optimizer, a breast-cancer classifier, a clinical workflow, a privacy mechanism, or a deployed platform. Instead, it establishes local contract controls and a documentation set that distinguish a planned synthetic FedProx study from an empirical result. [4] [5]

| Contribution | Evidence-bounded statement | Explicitly not claimed |
|---|---|---|
| C1. Conceptual distinction | A proposed synthetic FedProx study can be represented as non-executing study intent. | That a FedProx experiment was completed. |

# Chapter 2. Related Work and Positioning

Li and colleagues describe FedProx as a generalization and re-parameterization of FedAvg intended for systems and statistical heterogeneity in federated optimization. [1] That work explains why FedProx is a relevant protocol family to name in the thesis. It does not supply a performance value, convergence result, dataset, or comparison that can be attributed to this project. The present thesis does not reproduce FedProx experiments, configure a proximal term, instantiate a model, or execute an aggregation round.

## 2.2 Healthcare federated learning: technical activity is not clinical translation

Healthcare federated-learning literature reports substantial technical activity while distinguishing proof-of-concept work from real-world application. [2] That distinction is especially important in a breast-cancer research context, where technical design, lawful research access, operational integration, and clinical usefulness are separate evidence categories. This thesis occupies an earlier boundary still: it is a source-only contract and evidence-governance record, not a model-evaluation study or a healthcare prototype.

It is not a complete governance framework and does not substitute for institutional authorization, consent, ethics review, data stewardship, security assessment, or operational monitoring.

# Chapter 3. Methodology

## 3.1 Methodological positioning

This is a **documentation-first, source-only contract study**. Its unit of analysis is a frozen scalar declaration and associated scalar receipt, not a patient record, medical image, model weight, hyperparameter, metric value, or workflow run. The analytic outcome is whether the declaration is accepted as a proposed non-executing study posture or terminally closed. [5]

The method intentionally excludes data partitioning, model architecture, local training, inference, update packaging, submission, aggregation, external adapter behavior, and runtime invocation. Deterministic in-memory fixtures represent only allowlisted state classes and contain no dataset reference, model identifier, metric, credential, provider response, path, locator, or free-text diagnostic.

## 3.2 Source-only procedure

| Step | Action | Permitted evidence | Prohibited action or claim |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Declare | Create one exact, frozen proposed-study descriptor. | Scalar proposed/non-executing state. | Dataset/model selection, training start, or empirical protocol execution. |
| 2. Validate | Apply the source-only validator to the exact declaration. | Accepted or terminal-closure receipt. | Metric calculation, inference, or clinical interpretation. |
| 3. Consume once | Pass the receipt to a one-use consumer. | Scalar consumed or closed outcome. | Retry, replacement declaration, trainer invocation, update creation, or aggregation. |
| 4. Retain adjacent closure | Keep remote observation default-ineligible and unattempted. | Local scalar closure only. | Remote query, dispatch, cancellation, or workflow modification. |
| 5. Report | Record local quality and bounded contract behavior. | Claim-safe documentation. | Full release certification, deployment readiness, or empirical result. |

## 3.3 Analysis plan and interpretation rule

The analysis is conformance-oriented. It asks whether the documented controls accept only the intended frozen non-executing declaration and terminally close unsafe or replayed input. Reportable outcomes are limited to exact declaration acceptance, invalid-state closure, replay suppression, retained ineligible readiness, and retained non-observation posture. [4] [5]

No numeric performance analysis is part of this method. There are no accuracy, loss, F1, AUC, sensitivity, specificity, convergence, communication, fairness, or privacy values to calculate, compare, visualize, or interpret. The absence of those quantities is a scope consequence, not a missing table to be filled by estimation.

## 3.4 Validity, quality, and ethics considerations

Exact schemas, frozen values, one-use consumption, deterministic fixtures, terminal closure, replay suppression, and import isolation support construct validity for the narrow distinction between intent and execution. Local source-quality checks support claims about bounded modules only. They neither certify a release nor prove behavior across external storage, transport, identity, deployment, clinical, or runtime boundaries. [4] [6]

The study does not use human participants, patient records, medical images, or clinical workflows. It makes no ethics approval, consent, legal, security, or privacy-outcome claim. Any future activity involving such matters would require a separately governed record from the responsible parties.

# Chapter 4. Bounded Implementation and Traceability

## 4.1 Components within the source-only boundary

The implementation consists of local controls that remain non-executing by design. A source-only validator accepts only the exact frozen declaration class; a one-use consumer projects one scalar closure; an in-memory terminal ledger retains closure across a bounded restart simulation; and a disabled application fake projects no action-capable result. Adjacent remote-quality and readiness controls retain the same default-closed posture. The components are described here at a high level to preserve the boundary and avoid disclosing protected or operational implementation detail. [4] [6] [7]

| Control | Bounded role | Evidence category | Adjacent boundary not established |
|---|---|---|---|
| Study-intent validator | Distinguishes exact non-executing declarations from invalid input. | Local source-only contract validation. | Trainer, data/model access, metric, update, or aggregation action. |
| One-use consumer | Prevents a receipt from becoming reusable execution authorization. | Local source-only contract validation. | Persistent authority, scheduling, or external workflow action. |
| Terminal ledger | Retains terminal closure during deterministic in-memory restart simulation. | Local in-memory behavior. | External persistence, storage, or recovery behavior. |
| Disabled application fake | Projects a frozen non-executing scalar outcome. | Local deterministic fake behavior. | Transport, adapter, deployment, or runtime client behavior. |
| Readiness and observation closure | Retains unassigned/ineligible and unattempted postures. | Local source-only closure. | Authority, approval, remote state, or target interaction. |

# Chapter 5. Results and Discussion

## 5.1 Bounded local findings

The completed branch established a source-only distinction between a proposed synthetic FedProx study and an executed empirical result. Exact frozen, allowlisted declarations can be represented as non-executing study intent. Malformed, broadened, mutable, unknown, and replayed input can be terminally closed in the documented local contract scope. Pre-empirical readiness remains all-gates-unassigned and ineligible; the application projection remains disabled and in-memory only. [4] [6]

| Bounded finding | Thesis-safe interpretation | Not established |

| Unsafe or replayed input closes terminally. | Local replacement and replay are prevented within the documented controls. | That an external workflow or runtime is protected. |
| Remote observation remains unattempted and default-ineligible. | A local label cannot become workflow access through this branch. | A remote quality state or source-quality certification. |

## 5.3 Answer to the research question

Within the strict source-only scope, the answer is **yes**: a descriptor-only synthetic FedProx study-intent declaration can distinguish a proposed protocol from an empirical result before trainer, data, model, metric, update, and aggregation actions are introduced. This answer is limited to the behavior of the documented local contracts. It is not an answer to whether FedProx performs well, converges, protects privacy in operation, is fair, is clinically useful, or is deployable. [4] [6]

## 5.4 Discussion

The main value of the result is evidentiary discipline. It prevents a planned protocol from being presented as a completed federated-learning study and prevents local source controls from silently acquiring execution authority. The source-only controls do not solve the technical, governance, or translation challenges of healthcare federated learning. Instead, they make those unresolved requirements visible and preserve their status as separate decisions.

Published literature remains context, not transferred evidence. FedProx literature supports the selection of a proposed algorithmic context, healthcare federated-learning literature supports caution about translation, and governance literature supports the relevance of explicit controls. None of these sources supplies a result for the present project. [1] [2] [3]

# Chapter 6. Limitations, Future Work, and Conclusion

## 6.1 Limitations

The limitations follow directly from the source-only design. No dataset or model was accessed; therefore, the thesis cannot characterize data quality, preprocessing, model validity, generalizability, or predictive performance. No training or inference occurred; therefore, it cannot report optimization behavior or FedProx effectiveness. No metric pipeline was constructed; therefore, it contains no results table, statistical test, or model comparison. No remote workflow was observed; therefore, it cannot characterize remote quality-state behavior or workflow reliability. No hospital or clinical setting was evaluated; therefore, it cannot claim patient benefit, diagnostic utility, workflow fit, privacy outcome, or regulatory readiness. [5] [6]

## 6.2 Independently governed future work

Future work must begin outside the current branch and with independent governance—not by treating the current manuscript as implicit authorization. The first required activity is a bounded authority, ethics, legal, data-stewardship, and protocol determination by the responsible parties. That determination must define a permitted data and model boundary, research question, evaluation and statistical-analysis plan, reproducibility controls, safety controls, and result-reporting rules. [8] [10]

If those prerequisites are independently established, a separate concrete-adapter review may define ownership, identity, scope, redaction, non-mutating behavior, lifecycle controls, and proof preconditions. A concrete adapter, controlled execution, and any runtime proof must remain distinct later increments. Only evidence produced through a separately governed empirical study could support technical-performance, privacy, clinical-relevance, operational-reliability, or deployment-suitability claims.

| Required future decision area | Present manuscript status |
|---|---|
| Study authority and accountable ownership | Unassigned; not inferred from source-only controls. |
| Ethics, legal, consent, and data stewardship | Unassigned; no data or clinical activity occurred. |
| Data/model boundary and protocol | Unassigned; no dataset, model, or trainer was selected. |
| Metrics and statistical-analysis plan | Unassigned; no metric calculation occurred. |
| Reproducibility, safety, reporting, and retention | Unassigned; no empirical run or result exists. |
| Concrete adapter and runtime-proof design | Unassigned; no external adapter or runtime action occurred. |

## 6.3 Conclusion

This thesis addressed a narrow but consequential distinction in synthetic FedProx research: a proposed study protocol is not an executed empirical result. It demonstrated, at source-only contract scope, how frozen non-executing declarations, exact validation, one-use consumption, terminal closure, default-closed readiness, and claim traceability can preserve that distinction. [4] [5] [6]

The principal contribution is not a claimed performance gain. It is an accountable evidence boundary around a proposed FedProx study. By retaining the difference between intention, implementation, and evidence, the work makes later empirical research easier to govern, evaluate, and report without overstating what has been established. No empirical evaluation is reported; no dataset, patient record, medical image, or model artifact entered the documented source-only boundary; no training, inference, metric calculation, update, submission, or aggregation action occurred; and no privacy, clinical, hospital, operational, deployment, or runtime outcome is claimed. [9]
