# Synthetic FedProx Thesis Results and Discussion Draft

**Status:** Thesis-writing support draft. This document reports the completed source-only contract findings only. It does not report an empirical federated-learning result, dataset/model result, privacy outcome, clinical finding, hospital evaluation, deployment result, or runtime proof.

## Results

### Bounded result statement

The completed branch established a source-only distinction between a proposed synthetic FedProx study and an executed empirical result. The local contracts accept only exact frozen, allowlisted declarations and terminally close malformed, broadened, mutable, unknown, or replayed input. The adjacent pre-empirical readiness branch retains every gate as unassigned, produces ineligible and non-executing scalar outcomes, suppresses replay, and projects only a disabled in-memory application outcome.

> **Result interpretation:** These findings concern local contract and closure behavior. They do not demonstrate FedProx optimization behavior, model quality, data validity, privacy in operation, clinical utility, hospital integration, or deployment readiness.

### Absence of empirical results

No research dataset, patient record, medical image, model artifact, training run, inference, metric calculation, update package, submission, aggregation event, remote workflow query, target interaction, deployment, hospital system, or clinical workflow was part of this branch. Accordingly, there are no accuracy, loss, F1, AUC, sensitivity, specificity, convergence, fairness, communication, privacy, clinical, or operational metrics to report.

## Discussion

### Contribution

The central contribution is an evidence boundary: it makes the difference between **study intent** and **study result** explicit and enforceable at the source-contract level. This distinction is valuable for a thesis that uses FedProx as a proposed protocol context, because it prevents a planned research design from being presented as though it were an evaluated federated-learning system. The contract, closure, and traceability artifacts provide a disciplined way to describe what is known, what is locally validated, and what remains unknown.

The source-only controls do not solve the practical challenges of federated learning in healthcare. Rather, they ensure that those challenges are not silently bypassed in documentation or software state. An unassigned readiness state remains unassigned after validation, consumption, terminal recording, restart simulation, and disabled application projection. This provides a narrow governance mechanism against category error, not a comprehensive governance framework.

### Relationship to the thesis question

The results support the limited thesis question: a descriptor-only synthetic FedProx study-intent declaration can be used to distinguish a proposed protocol from an empirical result before trainer, data, model, metric, update, or aggregation actions are introduced. The evidence supports this conclusion only as a source-only contract statement. It does not answer whether FedProx performs well, is private, is fair, converges, or is clinically useful.

### Limitations

The results are constrained by design. The branch does not contain data, model, training, evaluation, or deployment evidence. Local source-quality validation is evidence about bounded modules, not a release or clinical certification. The in-memory terminal ledger and disabled application fake are not external persistence, transport, or runtime clients. The related-work literature supplies context for FedProx and healthcare federated-learning governance, but it does not transfer its empirical findings to this project.

### Implications for future work

Future work must proceed through independent governance, not through extension of the present results. A future empirical study would require separately assigned authority; lawful data and model stewardship; a prespecified protocol; metric and analysis plans; reproducibility, safety, and reporting controls; and a focused concrete-adapter review. Only evidence generated through those separately governed boundaries could support empirical, privacy, clinical, operational, or deployment claims.

## Suggested thesis conclusion sentence

> “This thesis demonstrates a source-only governance boundary for distinguishing proposed synthetic FedProx study intent from an executed empirical result; it does not report a federated-learning performance, privacy, clinical, or deployment outcome.”
